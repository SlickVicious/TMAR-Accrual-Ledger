AMENDMENT TO IFT — ARTICLE VI — MINOR PROTECTIVE SUB-TRUST PROVISION

Pursuant to the powers vested in the Trust Protector under Article VIII of the Infinivinity Family Trust, DOC-1818, and pursuant to Section 8.2(b), the Trust Protector hereby amends Article VI of the IFT by adding the following Section 6.8 immediately following Section 6.7 as added by DOC-0155-A.

Section 6.8 — Minor Protective Sub-Trust.

Section 6.8(a) — Automatic Creation. A Minor Protective Sub-Trust shall be automatically created within this Trust for each minor First Tier beneficiary upon the earlier of that beneficiary's birth or adoption, or the date on which the Trustee first proposes a distribution of IFT corpus or income to or for the benefit of that minor beneficiary. The Minor Protective Sub-Trust is a sub-account of the IFT and is not a separate legal entity. It has no separate employer identification number. All assets held in the Minor Protective Sub-Trust remain part of the IFT corpus.

Section 6.8(b) — Administration. The Trustee of the IFT serves ex officio as administrator of each Minor Protective Sub-Trust. The Trust Protector shall approve a distribution schedule for each Minor Protective Sub-Trust designating the age or ages at which corpus and income shall be distributed to the beneficiary, which age shall not be less than 21 years and shall not exceed the age established in the IFT distribution standards of Section 7.1. Until the beneficiary reaches the designated distribution age, the Trustee shall hold all IFT corpus allocated to that beneficiary in the Minor Protective Sub-Trust, investing and administering it for that beneficiary's health, education, maintenance, and support. The Trustee may make interim distributions from the Minor Protective Sub-Trust for the minor beneficiary's benefit in accordance with Section 7.1 as directed by the Trust Protector.

Section 6.8(c) — Disaffirmance of Minority Contracts. Any contract entered into by any minor beneficiary of this Trust before reaching the age of majority, including without limitation any service agreement, subscription, consumer contract, or financial obligation, may be disaffirmed by the Trustee on behalf of that minor beneficiary's interest in this Trust within a reasonable period after the minor reaches the age of majority. The Trustee's power of disaffirmance is a fiduciary power held for the protection of the minor beneficiary's equitable interest in the Trust corpus. It does not constitute a personal obligation of the Trustee. Any recovery resulting from the exercise of the power of disaffirmance shall be added to that beneficiary's Minor Protective Sub-Trust.

Section 6.8(d) — Termination of Minor Protective Sub-Trust. Upon a minor beneficiary reaching the distribution age approved by the Trust Protector, the Minor Protective Sub-Trust for that beneficiary terminates, and the corpus and income therein are distributed to that beneficiary in accordance with the approved distribution schedule. If the beneficiary dies before reaching the distribution age, the Minor Protective Sub-Trust corpus reverts to the general IFT corpus and is administered for the remaining beneficiary class under Section 7.6.

Section 6.8(e) — No Effect on Beneficiary Class. The establishment of a Minor Protective Sub-Trust for any beneficiary does not alter that beneficiary's standing as a First Tier beneficiary of the IFT. It is an administrative mechanism for managing that beneficiary's allocable share during minority and does not affect any other provision of this instrument.

DECLARATION OF PRIVATE STATUS AND NON-TAXPAYER POSITION — CLINTON WIMBERLY IV

I, Clinton Wimberly IV, a living man, a natural person of flesh and blood, born of woman, not a creature of statute, not a corporate fiction, and not an artificial entity, hereby make, execute, and deliver this Declaration of Private Status and Non-Taxpayer Position.

ARTICLE I — DECLARATION OF PRIVATE STATUS

Section 1.1 — Natural Person. I am a living man operating in my private and non-commercial capacity. I am not a public officer, a public employee, a corporate officer, or a person engaged in a federal activity, a federal instrumentality, or any activity subject to the jurisdiction of the United States as a governmental actor rather than as a constitutionally constrained republic of limited powers.

Section 1.2 — Indigenous Status. I am an indigenous person, a descendant of the original peoples of the land now known as the United States of America, as established and affirmed in my Affidavit of Indigenous Identity and Status, DOC-0139. My status as an indigenous person predates and is superior to any statutory classification imposed upon me without my knowing, voluntary, and informed consent.

Section 1.3 — Private Capacity. All trust instruments bearing my name as Settlor, Grantor, or Trustee are private common law instruments created in my private capacity and not in any commercial, public, or federal capacity. The A Provident Private Creditor Revocable Living Trust, EIN ending 9588, and the Infinivinity Family Trust, DOC-1818, are private common law express trusts, not entities created under any statute, not persons subject to federal income taxation as artificial entities, and not commercial actors within the federal commercial jurisdiction.

ARTICLE II — HISTORICAL AND LEGAL GROUNDING

Section 2.1 — Emergency Banking Relief Act. The Emergency Banking Relief Act, enacted by the Congress of the United States on March 9, 1933, expanded the Trading with the Enemy Act of October 6, 1917 to include the people of the United States as persons subject to federal emergency economic management. This expansion fundamentally altered the relationship between the natural person and the federal government. I hereby declare my awareness of this history and my election to operate in the private capacity that predated and survives that expansion.

Section 2.2 — Presidential Proclamation 2039. Presidential Proclamation 2039, issued by President Franklin D. Roosevelt on March 6, 1933, declared a national banking emergency and suspended normal commercial and financial operations. This Proclamation, together with the Emergency Banking Relief Act, constitutes the historical basis for the federal government's assertion of emergency jurisdiction over commercial and financial activity. I hereby declare that my private trust instruments and private estate are not subject to that emergency jurisdiction because they operate outside the commercial and financial activity that was the subject of those emergency declarations.

Section 2.3 — Constitutional Reservation. The Constitution for the United States, as originally ratified, reserves all powers not delegated to the federal government to the states and to the people. The Tenth Amendment and the Ninth Amendment confirm that the enumeration of certain rights shall not be construed to deny or disparage others retained by the people. My right to manage my private estate through private common law trust instruments without federal commercial interference is a right retained by me as one of the people.

Section 2.4 — United Nations Declaration. The United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples, Articles 9, 33, and 35, affirms my right as an indigenous person to maintain and develop my own institutional structures and to determine and develop priorities and strategies for the development or use of my resources. My private trust estate is an institutional structure developed by me in accordance with my inherent rights.

ARTICLE III — NON-TAXPAYER POSITION

Section 3.1 — Private Capacity Election. In my private capacity as a living man, I am not a taxpayer as that term is applied to persons engaged in federal activities, federal franchises, or the exercise of federal privileges. I pay all lawful taxes and assessments that apply to me as a matter of mandatory law. I do not waive any right to challenge the application of any tax, assessment, or levy that is not properly grounded in mandatory law applicable to a natural person in a private capacity.

Section 3.2 — Trust Tax Treatment. My revocable living trust, the APPCRLT, is a grantor trust for federal income tax purposes. All income of the APPCRLT is reportable on my individual federal income tax return. The trust does not file a separate return during my lifetime. This treatment is consistent with the grantor trust provisions of the applicable Revenue Acts and is not a concession that the trust itself is a taxpayer. The trust is a legal entity for property holding purposes and a grantor trust for income reporting purposes. These are not inconsistent characterizations.

Section 3.3 — IFT Tax Position. The IFT is structured as a grantor trust during my lifetime through the reimbursement power established in IFT Article XVIII. This structure allows the IFT to exist as an irrevocable trust for asset protection and estate planning purposes while the income flows through to my individual return. Upon my death or the release of the reimbursement power, the IFT becomes a separate taxpayer and files its own fiduciary income tax return. This transition is planned, documented, and consistent with applicable Revenue Act provisions.

ARTICLE IV — RESERVATION AND NON-WAIVER

Section 4.1 — All Rights Reserved. I expressly reserve all rights at law and in equity, including all rights arising under the common law of equity, the Constitution for the United States of America, the Acts of Congress published in the Statutes at Large, the common law of North Carolina, the law of nations, and the United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples. Nothing in this Declaration constitutes a waiver of any right, claim, defense, cause of action, or remedy. Nothing in this Declaration shall be construed as an admission of any liability, a submission to any jurisdiction not expressly consented to, or a concession that any tribunal, agency, or officer possesses authority over my person or property beyond that which the Constitution and laws of the United States of America lawfully confer.

STANDALONE FAITH-BASED ORGANIZATION — INFINIVINITY FAMILY MINISTRY — 508(c)(1)(a) DESIGNATION — OPTION B

Pursuant to the direction of the Settlor and Trust Protector of the Infinivinity Family Trust, the Charitable Sub-Trust established under DOC-0137-B is hereby elevated to a standalone faith-based organization under 508(c)(1)(a) of the Internal Revenue Code.

DECLARATION OF IRREVOCABLE TRUST AND ARTICLES OF FAITH-BASED ORGANIZATION

INFINIVINITY FAMILY MINISTRY

A FAITH-BASED ORGANIZATION AND 508(c)(1)(a) DESIGNATED TRUST

ARTICLE I — ORGANIZATION NAME AND DESIGNATION

Section 1.1 — Name. This organization shall be known as the Infinivinity Family Ministry, hereinafter designated the Ministry or IFM.

Section 1.2 — Legal Form. The IFM is organized as a common law irrevocable trust and faith-based organization under the 508(c)(1)(a) mandatory exception provisions of the Internal Revenue Code, which provide that churches, their integrated auxiliaries, and associations of churches are not required to apply for recognition of tax-exempt status and are not required to file annual information returns. The IFM operates as a faith-based organization within the meaning of 26 U.S.C. 508(c)(1)(a) and is entitled to all benefits, protections, and mandatory exceptions applicable to such organizations.

Section 1.3 — Relationship to IFT. The IFM is established as a standalone entity funded by grants from the Infinivinity Family Trust through the IFT Charitable Sub-Trust, DOC-0137-B. The IFM is not a sub-account of the IFT. It is a separate legal entity with its own governing instrument, its own EIN, and its own fiduciary structure. The IFT Charitable Sub-Trust directs grants to the IFM as a qualifying recipient under the terms of DOC-0137-B and DOC-1830-02.

ARTICLE II — FAITH DECLARATION AND TENETS

Section 2.1 — Foundational Declaration. We, the founders of this Ministry, believe that every human being is created in the image of the Creator and endowed with inherent dignity, sovereignty, and unalienable rights. We affirm unconditional love, unlimited forgiveness, undying hope, and the sacred duty of stewardship over all entrusted resources.

Section 2.2 — Tenets. We believe in the sovereignty of the individual living man as a creation of the Most High. We believe in the inherent rights of indigenous peoples to determine their own identity, governance, and institutional structures. We believe in the sanctity of the family as the foundational unit of civilization. We believe in the obligation of stewardship over wealth, resources, and knowledge for the benefit of present and future generations.

ARTICLE III — PURPOSE

Section 3.1 — Charitable and Religious Purpose. The IFM is organized and operated exclusively for charitable, educational, and religious purposes within the meaning of 508(c)(1)(a) of the Internal Revenue Code. Its purposes include the provision of shelter, food, clothing, and care to persons in need; the advancement of education in financial literacy, bookkeeping, and accounting for its members and beneficiaries; the promotion of vocational training and entrepreneurial development; the support of indigenous sovereignty and cultural preservation; and the administration of grant programs for qualified recipients.

Section 3.2 — No Private Benefit. No part of the net earnings of the IFM shall inure to the benefit of any private individual. Reasonable compensation for services rendered is permitted. No pecuniary profit shall accrue to any member or officer except as reasonable compensation for services.

ARTICLE IV — GOVERNANCE

Section 4.1 — Trustee. The initial Trustee of the IFM is [INSERT TRUSTEE FULL LEGAL NAME], serving in the capacity of Trustee and not individually.

Section 4.2 — Trust Protector. The Trust Protector of the IFM is the Trust Protector of the Infinivinity Family Trust serving ex officio, in the same manner as the ex officio Protector designation established in DOC-0137-A and DOC-0137-B. The Trust Protector of the IFM approves all grant distributions, monitors compliance with the 508(c)(1)(a) requirements, and coordinates administration between the IFM and the IFT.

Section 4.3 — Board of Directors. The Board of Directors shall consist of no fewer than three and no more than five individuals. A majority of the Board shall be members of the IFM community. No more than two Board members shall be related to each other.

Section 4.4 — Self-Dealing Prohibition. The IFM shall not engage in any act of self-dealing. No Trustee, Board member, or Trust Protector shall receive any grant, distribution, or benefit from the IFM other than reasonable compensation for services rendered.

ARTICLE V — DISSOLUTION

Section 5.1 — Dissolution Distribution. Upon dissolution of the IFM, all assets shall be distributed to one or more organizations qualifying under 501(c)(3) and 508(c)(1)(a) of the Internal Revenue Code, or to the Infinivinity Family Trust Charitable Sub-Trust, DOC-0137-B, for continued charitable distribution in accordance with the IFT's governing instrument.

ARTICLE VI — GOVERNING LAW

Section 6.1 — Governing Authority. This organization is governed by the common law of equity and the faith-based organization provisions of the Internal Revenue Code, particularly 508(c)(1)(a). The courts of the State of North Carolina shall be the exclusive forum for any proceeding arising out of or relating to this organization.
