This analysis evaluates every instrument in the estate package against the strategic objectives you have identified. The analysis applies your principles, context, parts, and persons throughout. The structure is then mapped against the functional model you described, enhanced with the specific provisions, entities, and legal grounding already built into your instruments.

PART ONE — THE COMPLETE ESTATE ARCHITECTURE AS IT STANDS

The estate is composed of two parallel family structures that share a common legal design but operate independently under separate Settlors, separate trusts, separate domiciles, and separate governing instruments.

CLINTON WIMBERLY IV — NORTH CAROLINA ESTATE

The master irrevocable dynasty trust is the IFT. It is the Mega Trust. The APPCRLT is the revocable living trust that serves as the operational vehicle during the Settlor's lifetime. It is the Micro Trust analog. The IFT holds the Business Sub-Trust DOC-0137-A as the commercial operating arm. The IFT holds the Charitable Sub-Trust DOC-0137-B as the philanthropic arm. The pour-over Will DOC-1833 captures any probate assets and directs them to the APPCRLT, which then pours to the IFT. The Durable General POA DOC-1831 maintains administrative continuity during incapacity. The Healthcare POA DOC-1832 governs medical decisions. The Living Will DOC-1835 establishes terminal care directives. The Trustee Resolution DOC-1834 coordinates execution.

SHAWN SYLVESTER WIMBERLY — VIRGINIA ESTATE

The master irrevocable dynasty trust is the CC. It is the Mega Trust. The VV is the revocable living trust that serves as the operational vehicle during the Settlor's lifetime. It is the Micro Trust analog. The CC holds the Business Sub-Trust DOC-0137-A adapted for CC as the commercial operating arm. The CC holds the Charitable Sub-Trust DOC-0137-B adapted for CC as the philanthropic arm. The pour-over Will DOC-1822 captures probate assets and directs them to the VV, which then pours to the CC. The Durable General POA DOC-1820 governs administrative continuity. The Healthcare POA DOC-1821 governs medical decisions. The Living Will DOC-1824 establishes terminal care directives. The Trustee Resolution DOC-1823 coordinates execution.

PART TWO — FUNCTIONAL POSITIONING ANALYSIS — IS EVERY INSTRUMENT STRATEGICALLY PLACED

The Mega Trust position is correctly occupied. The IFT and CC are irrevocable, dynasty-structured, common law express trusts with multi-tiered beneficiary classes, spendthrift protection, no-contest clauses, grantor trust toggle, Crummey withdrawal rights, insurance policy provisions, sub-trust creation authority, and a single Trust Protector serving all sub-arms ex officio. This is the correct position for the master wealth holding instrument. It is the apex of the structure and it is bulletproofed at every pressure point.

The Micro Trust position is correctly occupied. The APPCRLT and VV are revocable during lifetime, grantor trusts for tax purposes, primary beneficiary vehicles for the Settlor, and pour-over conduits at death. They hold the Settlor's daily life accounts through the account retitling package. They are the operational layer. The retitling package as reviewed in DOC-0149 through DOC-0151 is the mechanism by which daily obligations, services, and subscriptions are brought inside the trust structure and away from the Settlor's personal name.

The sub-trust arms are correctly positioned. The Business Sub-Trust is a sub-account of the Mega Trust, not a separate entity. It has no separate EIN, no separate fiduciary, no separate Protector. The IFT or CC Protector serves ex officio. This eliminates administrative fragmentation and keeps all commercial operations under the Mega Trust's spendthrift protection, no-contest clause, and asset protection framework.

The charitable arm is correctly positioned. The Charitable Sub-Trust is a sub-account of the Mega Trust, grant-making is directed by the Trust Protector, and all assets revert to the Mega Trust corpus on dissolution. This eliminates the need for a separate FBO entity with independent board, membership, and IRS filings.

The personal-capacity instruments are correctly positioned. The POAs, Healthcare POA, and Living Will create a complete incapacity framework that preserves the Settlor's estate plan during any period of disability without requiring court intervention. The Pour-Over Will closes the probate gap, ensuring that any asset that escapes the trust during the Settlor's lifetime is captured at death and directed into the trust chain.

PART THREE — GAPS AND STRATEGIC ENHANCEMENTS REQUIRED

The following enhancements are required to achieve the full functionality model you described.

GAP ONE — THE MEGA TRUST BENEFICIARY DESIGNATION FOR THE MICRO TRUST

As identified in DOC-0153, the IFT does not currently contain a provision formally designating the APPCRLT as a permissible lifetime distribution vehicle. The Settlor is the primary beneficiary of the IFT under Section 6.1 but distributions to the Settlor are directed through the IFT to the Settlor personally, not through the APPCRLT as a conduit. A provision should be added to IFT Article VI stating that distributions from the IFT to the Settlor during his lifetime may be made to the APPCRLT as the Settlor's designated revocable trust vehicle, and that such distributions to the APPCRLT shall be treated as distributions to the Settlor for all purposes of the IFT. This formally closes the loop between the Mega and Micro structures.

GAP TWO — THE TRUST WITHIN A TRUST STRUCTURE FOR MINOR ESTATE PROTECTION

The IFT contains sub-trust creation authority in Section 7.4 but does not contain a dedicated minor beneficiary protection sub-trust provision parallel to the functional model you described. The following provision must be added to the IFT and CC.

Each minor First Tier beneficiary shall have a Minor Protective Sub-Trust automatically created within the IFT upon the earlier of that beneficiary's birth, adoption, or receipt of any IFT distribution. The Minor Protective Sub-Trust shall hold all IFT corpus allocated to that beneficiary until that beneficiary reaches the age designated by the Trustee in the Trust Protector-approved distribution schedule, which shall not be less than 21 years and shall default to the distribution schedule established in the IFT's Section 7.1. The Trustee shall serve as the trustee of each Minor Protective Sub-Trust. The Trust Protector shall approve the distribution schedule for each sub-trust. All Minor Protective Sub-Trusts are sub-accounts of the IFT and have no separate EIN, no separate filing obligations, and no separate trustee. They are encapsulated within the IFT corpus and administered as internal accounts.

GAP THREE — DISAFFIRMANCE OF CONTRACTS MADE WHILE IN MINORITY

No instrument in the current package contains a provision addressing the disaffirmance of contracts entered into by minor beneficiaries. The following provision must be added to both the IFT and CC.

Any contract entered into by any minor beneficiary of this Trust before reaching the age of majority, including without limitation any service agreement, subscription, consumer contract, or financial obligation, may be disaffirmed by the Trustee on behalf of the minor beneficiary's interest in this Trust within a reasonable period after the minor reaches the age of majority. The Trustee's power of disaffirmance is a fiduciary power held for the protection of the minor beneficiary's equitable interest in the Trust corpus and shall not constitute a personal obligation of the Trustee. Any recovery resulting from the exercise of the power of disaffirmance shall be added to the minor beneficiary's Minor Protective Sub-Trust.

GAP FOUR — REVOCATION OF ALL PREVIOUS POWERS OF ATTORNEY

The Durable General POA DOC-1831 and DOC-1820 each state that the Settlor grants this power of attorney to the Agent designated herein but neither contains an express revocation of all prior powers of attorney. An express revocation clause must be added to each POA as Article II Section 2.3 as follows.

This Power of Attorney expressly revokes any and all prior powers of attorney granted by the Principal in any capacity, whether general, limited, durable, springing, healthcare, or any other form, that predate the execution of this instrument. Any person or institution in possession of a prior power of attorney granted by the Principal is hereby placed on notice that such instrument is void and of no further force or effect as of the date of execution of this instrument. The Agent designated herein is authorized to deliver written notice of this revocation to any person or institution relying on a prior power of attorney.

The functional model you referenced includes a Non-Taxpayer Status position for the natural person in their private capacity, grounded in Presidential Proclamation 2039 and the Emergency Banking Relief Act enacted by Congress in 1933. The Affidavit of Indigenous Identity, DOC-0139, is the foundation for this position. The following additional instrument must be produced to complete this layer.

This instrument does not appear in the current package and must be drafted separately.

GAP SIX — ESTATE EIN, SOLE PROPRIETOR EIN, AND BANKRUPTCY ESTATE EIN

The current package assigns one EIN to the APPCRLT ending in 9588 and requires a separate EIN for the IFT upon assignment. The functional model you described requires three EIN positions.

The Estate EIN is the IFT EIN, to be obtained upon execution of the IFT. This is planned and the process is documented.

The Sole Proprietor EIN would be a separate EIN for any sole proprietorship or Schedule C business activity conducted by the Settlor in his personal capacity before or alongside the trust structure. This has not been addressed in the current package. If the Settlor operates any sole proprietorship, a separate EIN must be obtained for that entity and it must be listed in Schedule A of the APPCRLT as a business interest transferred to the trust.

The Bankruptcy Estate EIN is a specialized identifier that arises only if a bankruptcy proceeding is commenced. It is not a proactive EIN that must be obtained now. It arises by operation of law upon filing. No action is required on this point unless and until a bankruptcy filing is contemplated.

GAP SEVEN — NONPROFIT RELIGIOUS TAX-EXEMPT REGISTRATION

The Charitable Sub-Trust as currently structured operates as a sub-account of the IFT without a separate 508(c)(1)(a) designation. The FBO template uploaded earlier in these sessions was a standalone 508(c)(1)(a) entity. As restructured, the charitable function is absorbed into the IFT Charitable Sub-Trust and operates without a separate registration. If the Settlor wishes to obtain a standalone 508(c)(1)(a) designation for the charitable arm, the charitable sub-trust must be established as a separate entity outside the IFT rather than as a sub-account. This is a design choice that must be made before production proceeds. The options are as follows.

Option A. Retain the Charitable Sub-Trust as a sub-account of the IFT with no separate registration, no separate EIN, and no separate IRS filing. This is the current design. It is administratively simpler but does not produce a standalone nonprofit designation.

Option B. Establish a separate 508(c)(1)(a) faith-based organization as a standalone entity, funded by grants from the IFT Charitable Sub-Trust, with its own EIN and its own governance. This is the original FBO template design. It requires a separate board, separate bylaws, and separate administration, but it produces a standalone nonprofit designation.

The choice between Option A and Option B must be made before the charitable arm instruments are finalized.

PART FOUR — THE FIVE-ENTITY MINIMUM REQUIREMENT

The functional model requires a minimum of five entities: Grantor, Beneficiary, Trustee, Trust Protector, and Fiduciary. The current structure meets and exceeds this requirement as follows.

In the IFT, the Grantor is Clinton Wimberly IV. The Initial Trustee is a designated third party independent of the Settlor. The Trust Protector is a designated third party independent of the Trustee. The beneficiary class is a three-tiered structure encompassing the entire Wimberly family. The Fiduciary layer is further populated by the IFT Protector serving ex officio over the Business Sub-Trust and the Charitable Sub-Trust, the General Manager of the Business Sub-Trust appointed under DOC-1830-01, and the grant-making director of the Charitable Sub-Trust, which is the Protector in that capacity.

In the APPCRLT, the Settlor and initial Trustee are the same person, Clinton Wimberly IV. The Trust Protector is a designated third party. The beneficiary class mirrors the IFT. This is a point of structural attention. The APPCRLT as a revocable grantor trust where the Settlor is also the Trustee does not maintain strict separation of the Grantor and Trustee entities. This is legally permissible for a revocable trust but it means the APPCRLT does not independently satisfy the five-entity minimum if the Grantor and Trustee are the same person. The IFT satisfies the five-entity minimum independently because the Initial Trustee is not the Settlor.
