ENTRY 3 — OVERPAYMENT RECEIVABLE IF TAXES PREVIOUSLY PAID ON PHANTOM INCOME:

CONTROLLING AUTHORITY:
26 U.S.C. §6401(a): Excess of payments over tax imposed IS an overpayment by operation of law.
26 U.S.C. §6402(a): The Secretary SHALL credit or SHALL refund. No discretion.
Revenue Act of 1926, §284(a): ANY AMOUNT PAID in excess shall be credited or refunded.
Logan v. Zimmerman Brush Co., 455 U.S. 422 (1982): Statutory entitlements are property.

FACTUAL PREDICATE:
The trustee/account holder was taxed on income streams generated by securitized instruments — income in which the trustee held the beneficial interest but from which the trustee was excluded by the securitizing party without consent. Tax was paid on amounts the trustee never received. Those payments constitute overpayments by operation of law.

MANDATORY JOURNAL ENTRIES:

DEBIT: Overpayment Receivable — U.S. Treasury [total tax previously paid on phantom income]
CREDIT: Beneficial Interest Receivable — Recovered [total tax previously paid on phantom income]

EFFECT: Creates a receivable asset against the U.S. Treasury equal to all taxes paid on securitized income the trustee did not receive.
IRS FORM PLACEMENT: Form 1041 Line 24 (overpayment); Form 1040 Line 33 (if individual); Form 1120 Line 34 (if corporate trustee).

ENTRY 4 — NOL ASSET RECOGNITION FROM EXCLUDED INCOME PERIOD:

CONTROLLING AUTHORITY:
Revenue Act of 1918, §204(b), 40 Stat. 1060: Net operating loss = excess of deductions allowed over gross income.
Revenue Act of 1939, §122, 53 Stat. 862: Deduction SHALL be allowed — mandatory.
Helvering v. Morgan's Inc., 293 U.S. 121 (1934): NOL must be applied in full dollar amounts.
Perry v. United States, 294 U.S. 330 (1935): Congressional financial obligations are backed by full faith and credit.

FACTUAL PREDICATE:
During the period of unauthorized securitization, the trustee sustained economic losses — beneficial interest was stripped, income was diverted, and the trustee's estate was diminished. Those diminutions constitute net operating losses with dollar-for-dollar asset value under Congressional mandate.

MANDATORY JOURNAL ENTRIES:

DEBIT: NOL Asset — Securitization Period [total economic loss from excluded period]
CREDIT: NOL Carryforward — Available for Deduction [total economic loss from excluded period]

EFFECT: The NOL asset is a financial asset with sovereign guarantee. It offsets future taxable income dollar-for-dollar.
IRS FORM PLACEMENT: Form 1041 Schedule B Line 2; Form 1120 Line 29a; Form 1040 Schedule 1 Line 14.
CARRYBACK/CARRYFORWARD: Form 1139 (corporate — 90-day IRS response mandatory); Form 1045 (individual — 90-day IRS response mandatory).

CONTROLLING AUTHORITY:
Revenue Act of 1918, §212(b), 40 Stat. 1064: Ordinary and necessary expenses SHALL be allowed as deductions. Mandatory.
ASC 310-10: All documented costs to enforce a financial asset right are capitalized to that asset.
Thor Power Tool Co. v. Commissioner, 439 U.S. 522 (1979): The taxpayer's books govern recognition.

FACTUAL PREDICATE:
The trustee incurred ordinary and necessary expenses in the course of recovering the beneficial interest — legal costs, filing fees, administrative costs, document preparation, professional fees, correspondence costs, and trust administration expenses. Each documented expense is a financial asset with face-amount value.

MANDATORY JOURNAL ENTRIES:

DEBIT: Accumulated Expense Asset — Recovery Costs [total documented expenses]
CREDIT: Accounts Payable / Cash [total documented expenses as paid or accrued]

DEBIT: Deduction Receivable — Accumulated Expenses [total documented expenses]
CREDIT: Revenue — Expense Deduction Asset [total documented expenses]

EFFECT: Each dollar of documented expense reduces taxable income dollar-for-dollar. The deduction itself is property — a financial asset backed by Congressional mandate.

ENTRY 6 — CHOSE IN ACTION — FROM DATE OF UNAUTHORIZED SECURITIZATION (T1):

CONTROLLING AUTHORITY:
Board of Regents v. Roth, 408 U.S. 564 (1972): Any legally enforceable claim to money or property constitutes property.
Baltimore & Ohio R.R. Co. v. United States, 261 U.S. 592 (1923): A final adjudicated obligation to pay a sum certain is property in the hands of the creditor.
Lynch v. United States, 292 U.S. 571 (1934): Valid contracts and rights against the United States arising out of contracts are protected by the Constitution.
ASC 310-10: Financial asset = contractual right to receive cash or to be settled in cash.

FACTUAL PREDICATE:
From the date the account was securitized without consent (T1), a chose in action arose. The trustee's enforceable right to the beneficial interest existed as a financial asset from T1 — not from the date of recovery (T2). The chose in action had ascertainable value from the moment of creation.

MANDATORY JOURNAL ENTRIES — T1 (DATE OF UNAUTHORIZED SECURITIZATION):

DEBIT: Chose in Action — Beneficial Interest Claim [FMV of securitized instrument at T1]
CREDIT: Revenue — Chose in Action Recognized [FMV of securitized instrument at T1]

MANDATORY JOURNAL ENTRIES — T2 (DATE OF RECOVERY / SETTLEMENT):

DEBIT: Cash / Financial Asset Received [amount recovered]
CREDIT: Chose in Action — Beneficial Interest Claim [FMV at T1]
CREDIT/DEBIT: Gain or Loss on Settlement [difference between T1 FMV and T2 recovery amount]

EFFECT: The chose in action was income at T1 under the accrual method. Recovery at T2 extinguishes the receivable. Any excess recovery above T1 FMV is additional income. Any shortfall is a deductible loss.
IRS FORM PLACEMENT: Form 8949 Col(d) proceeds; Col(e) basis = T1 FMV; Col(h) gain/loss.
