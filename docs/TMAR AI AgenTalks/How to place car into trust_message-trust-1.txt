AUTOMOBILE TRANSFER INTO REVOCABLE LIVING TRUST — COMPLETE PROTOCOL


STEP 1 — IDENTIFY WHAT YOU ACTUALLY HAVE

A Certificate of Trust is NOT a title document.

| Document | What It Is | What It Does |
|----------|-----------|--------------|
| Certificate of Trust | Summary of trust existence and trustee authority | Proves the trust exists and the trustee has power to hold title |
| Certificate of Title (MCO/MSO) | State-issued ownership document | IS the legal instrument of ownership for a motor vehicle |

CONCLUSION OF LAW: The Certificate of Title — not the Certificate of Trust — is the instrument that must be transferred. The Certificate of Trust is the instrument that authorizes the trustee to RECEIVE the transfer.


STEP 2 — LOCATE THE TITLE

You must locate the physical Certificate of Title for the vehicle.

If you have possession of the title:
→ Proceed to Step 3.

If the vehicle is financed (lienholder holds title):
→ The lienholder holds the title. You cannot transfer without lienholder consent or payoff.
→ Contact the lienholder. Request written authorization to transfer title to the trust while maintaining the lien.
→ Some lenders will endorse the transfer. Some will not. This is a contractual matter between you and the lender.

If the title is lost:
→ Apply for a duplicate title at your state DMV before any transfer can occur.
→ Required: Vehicle Identification Number (VIN), proof of identity, duplicate title fee.


STEP 3 — THE TRANSFER INSTRUMENT

The Certificate of Title itself is the transfer instrument for a motor vehicle.

On the back of the Certificate of Title:

| Field | What to Enter |
|-------|--------------|
| Seller/Transferor | Your name exactly as it appears on the front of the title |
| Buyer/Transferee | Clinton Wimberly IV, Trustee of A Provident Private Creditor, a Trust dated [execution date] |
| Odometer Disclosure | Required by federal law — 49 U.S.C. §32705 — must be completed accurately |
| Signature | Grantor/current owner signs as seller |
| Notarization | Required in most states — check your state's specific requirement |

CRITICAL — TRUSTEE VESTING LANGUAGE:

The title must vest in the TRUSTEE — not the trust itself. A trust is not a legal person. The trustee holds title in their fiduciary capacity.

CORRECT VESTING:
Clinton Wimberly IV, Trustee of A Provident Private Creditor, dated [execution date]

INCORRECT — WILL BE REJECTED:
A Provident Private Creditor Trust ← trust cannot hold title directly in most states


STEP 4 — SUBMIT TO STATE DMV

Take the following to your state DMV:

| Document | Purpose |
|----------|---------|
| Signed Certificate of Title (back completed) | Primary transfer instrument |
| Certificate of Trust | Proves trustee authority — DMV will not require full trust document |
| Government-issued ID | Identity verification |
| Applicable transfer fee | Varies by state |
| Odometer disclosure statement | Federal requirement — 49 U.S.C. §32705 |

NOTE: Most states exempt transfers into a revocable living trust from:
Sales tax (grantor remains beneficial owner — no taxable sale has occurred)
Reassessment (no change of beneficial ownership under state property tax rules)

Present the Certificate of Trust to invoke this exemption where applicable.


STEP 5 — NEW TITLE ISSUED

The DMV will issue a new Certificate of Title reflecting:

Clinton Wimberly IV, Trustee of A Provident Private Creditor, dated [execution date]

This new title IS the completed transfer. The automobile is now a trust asset held in the trustee's fiduciary capacity.


ACCOUNTING TREATMENT — TRANSFER INTO TRUST

EVENT TYPE: Asset transfer — grantor to trustee (fiduciary capacity). No gain or loss event because:
Revocable living trust = grantor trust under IRC §676
Grantor remains the owner for federal income tax purposes
Transfer is disregarded for federal tax purposes

JOURNAL ENTRY — TRUST BOOKS:

| Entry | Account | Amount |
|-------|---------|--------|
| DEBIT | Trust Asset — Automobile (FMV at transfer date) | $[FMV] |
| CREDIT | Grantor Capital Contribution | $[FMV] |

IRS REPORTING: No Form 1099 required. No gain recognized. No taxable event. Grantor trust rules under IRC §671-677 apply. The asset continues to be reported on the grantor's personal return as if the trust does not exist for tax purposes.


REBUTTALS

PRESUMPTION: "The Certificate of Trust transfers the automobile."
REBUTTAL: The Certificate of Trust is evidence of trust existence and trustee authority. It is NOT a title instrument. Under every state's vehicle code, title to a motor vehicle passes by endorsement and delivery of the Certificate of Title — not by the Certificate of Trust.

PRESUMPTION: "The trust itself can appear on the title."
REBUTTAL: A trust is not a legal person capable of holding title. The trustee holds title in their fiduciary capacity. The vesting must name the individual trustee plus their fiduciary designation.

PRESUMPTION: "This transfer triggers sales tax or reassessment."
REBUTTAL: A revocable living trust is a grantor trust. The grantor remains the beneficial owner. No change in beneficial ownership has occurred. The transfer is tax-exempt in virtually every jurisdiction for this reason.


SUMMARY — REQUIRED ACTIONS IN ORDER
Locate Certificate of Title
If lost → apply for duplicate at DMV
If lienholder holds title → obtain lienholder consent
Complete back of Certificate of Title with trustee vesting language
Bring title + Certificate of Trust + ID + fee to DMV
Receive new title in trustee name
Book asset in trust records at FMV
No federal tax reporting required (grantor trust — disregarded)
