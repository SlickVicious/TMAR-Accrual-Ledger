PRIORITY FOUR — I.C. SYSTEM COLLECTION INQUIRY


Background and Legal Posture

I.C. System, Inc. is a debt collection company incorporated in Minnesota and operating nationally as a third-party debt collector. Its appearance as a soft inquiry coded as a collection inquiry on the TransUnion file dated December 6, 2025, establishes that I.C. System pulled the consumer's credit file in connection with a debt collection effort. Collection inquiries are distinct from promotional or account review inquiries. A collection inquiry means I.C. System was either retained by a creditor to collect a specific debt owed by this consumer, or it purchased a debt obligation assigned from a creditor and was conducting a locate or account review pull in preparation for collection activity.

No I.C. System tradeline appears anywhere in the file as of August 10, 2026. This means one of three things. First, I.C. System may not yet have placed a tradeline because it is still in the pre-reporting phase of its collection effort. Second, it may have been retained but the underlying debt may have been resolved, recalled, or transferred before a tradeline was posted. Third, the inquiry may belong to a mixed file and relates to a different consumer entirely.

The December 6, 2025, date is significant. It is the same date as an SSA Option 6 inquiry appearing on the TransUnion file. The SSA Option 6 code denotes a Social Security Administration identity verification or benefit inquiry, which is typically associated with credit applications or government benefit determinations. The simultaneous appearance of a collection inquiry and a government identity verification pull on the same date warrants scrutiny.


Legal Framework

I.C. System is a debt collector within the meaning of 15 U.S.C. § 1692a(6) of the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act. As such, it is obligated upon written request from the consumer to cease collection activity and provide verification of the debt under 15 U.S.C. § 1692g(b). The consumer's right to demand verification is not conditioned on the existence of a tradeline. The collection inquiry itself, combined with the absence of any prior written notice of the debt, is itself a potential violation. Under 15 U.S.C. § 1692g(a), a debt collector must, within five days of its initial communication with a consumer in connection with the collection of a debt, send the consumer a written notice containing the amount of the debt, the name of the creditor to whom the debt is owed, and the consumer's right to dispute the debt within 30 days. If I.C. System has not sent that notice and has already pulled the consumer's credit file, it may have made an initial communication through the credit pull without sending the required disclosure.

Additionally, pulling a consumer's credit report in connection with a debt collection effort without a permissible purpose under 15 U.S.C. § 1681b constitutes a violation of the Fair Credit Reporting Act. Collection of a debt is a permissible purpose, but only if the debt actually belongs to this consumer. If the inquiry relates to a mixed file or an account that does not belong to the consumer, the pull is impermissible and actionable.


Documents to Produce

The team will produce two instruments for Priority Four.

Document One is a Debt Validation Demand addressed to I.C. System, Inc. pursuant to 15 U.S.C. § 1692g(b), demanding complete identification of the alleged debt, the name and address of the original creditor, the date the debt was incurred, the amount claimed, the chain of assignment from the original creditor to I.C. System, and a copy of any written agreement bearing the consumer's signature. The demand will invoke the consumer's right to dispute the debt in its entirety and will direct I.C. System to cease all collection activity until verification is provided. The demand will simultaneously notify I.C. System that its December 6, 2025, credit inquiry has been identified and will be scrutinized for permissible purpose compliance.

Document Two is a concurrent dispute letter to TransUnion disputing the I.C. System inquiry on the ground that no permissible purpose has been established and demanding removal of the inquiry from the consumer file if I.C. System cannot demonstrate a lawful basis for the pull.

The mailing address for I.C. System, Inc. is P.O. Box 64378, Saint Paul, Minnesota 55164. The registered agent address for legal process is I.C. System, Inc., 444 Highway 96 East, Saint Paul, Minnesota 55127.

No additional operator input is required to produce these documents. The team will produce them.


PRIORITY FIVE — MEDICAL DEBT RESOLUTION INQUIRIES


Background and Legal Posture

The TransUnion file reflects two inquiries from an entity identified as Medical Debt Resolut, associated with ECMedical Debt Resolution, located in Long Island City, New York. The inquiry dates are July 8, 2026, and April 27, 2026. No medical collection tradeline appears anywhere in the file as of August 10, 2026.

Medical Debt Resolution is a company that provides revenue cycle management and debt resolution services to healthcare providers. Its appearance twice in a six-week span suggests that a healthcare provider retained it to evaluate or pursue a specific outstanding balance. The escalating frequency from April to July 2026 is consistent with a pre-collection escalation pattern in which the servicer is confirming the consumer's identity and creditworthiness before placing the account for active collection.


Critical Legal Development

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau finalized a rule effective March 17, 2025, that removes medical debt from credit reports and prohibits consumer reporting agencies from including medical debt information in consumer reports used for credit decisions. The rule was published in the Federal Register at 90 Fed. Reg. 3276. However, as of the date of this analysis, that rule faces legal challenge and the reporting prohibition has not been universally implemented. The operator should not rely on that rule as protection against a medical tradeline appearing on the credit file, as its enforceability remains unsettled.

More practically, the traditional protection is that medical debts under $500 were excluded from FICO Score 8 and subsequent models, and all paid medical debts were excluded by the major bureaus under their voluntary Medical Debt Credit Reporting Changes agreement effective July 1, 2022. However, an unpaid medical debt above $500 can still post to the credit file after a 12-month waiting period from the date of the first delinquency.


Action Required

The operator must immediately take two steps outside of document production. First, identify whether any medical bill from any provider is currently outstanding and unpaid. Contact each healthcare provider used in the 12 months preceding April 2026 and confirm whether any balance has been referred to Medical Debt Resolution or any other collection intermediary. Second, if a balance is identified, pay it directly to the provider before it posts as a tradeline, as payment to the provider rather than to the collector ensures that the collection entry never appears on the file.

If the operator cannot identify the underlying provider or the outstanding balance, the team will produce a written inquiry addressed to Medical Debt Resolution LLC, Long Island City, New York, demanding identification of the creditor client on whose behalf the inquiry was made, the amount of the alleged debt, the date of service, and the consumer's right to validation under applicable law. The FDCPA applies to medical debt collectors in the same manner as commercial debt collectors.

The team requires the following information to produce that inquiry letter: the full legal mailing address of Medical Debt Resolution LLC in Long Island City, New York. The inquiry address is not in the credit file. Once confirmed, the letter will be produced immediately.


PRIORITY SIX — ADVERSE ACTION NOTICES


Background and Legal Posture

The following institutions appear in the soft inquiry sections of the credit files without a corresponding tradeline, indicating either a credit application that did not result in an account or an account review that the operator did not initiate.

Bank of America appears as a regular inquiry on TransUnion dated March 15, 2026, coded as an individual inquiry, and as multiple soft inquiries on Experian from September 2025 through July 2026. No Bank of America tradeline is in the file.

Goldman Sachs Bank USA, the issuer of Apple Card, appears as an account review inquiry on TransUnion on September 10, 2025. No Apple Card tradeline is in the file.

Navy Federal Credit Union appears as a soft inquiry on TransUnion dated June 16, 2025. No Navy Federal tradeline is in the file.

Under the Equal Credit Opportunity Act, 15 U.S.C. § 1691 et seq., and its implementing regulation at 12 C.F.R. Part 1002, a creditor must provide an adverse action notice to an applicant whose application for credit is denied, within 30 days of the decision. The notice must state the specific reasons for the denial or inform the applicant of the right to obtain the reasons within 60 days. Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act, 15 U.S.C. § 1681m, if a consumer reporting agency report was a factor in the adverse action decision, the notice must include the name, address, and telephone number of the consumer reporting agency, a statement that the agency did not make the adverse action decision and cannot provide the reasons for it, and notice of the consumer's right to obtain a free copy of the report and to dispute its accuracy.


Operator Determination Required

The team cannot determine from the credit file alone whether applications were submitted to these institutions. The operator must confirm whether credit applications were submitted to Bank of America, Goldman Sachs Bank through the Apple Card platform, or Navy Federal Credit Union. If applications were submitted and denials were received, the adverse action notices should already be in the operator's possession. If they were not retained, the operator may request them directly from each institution in writing.

If the operator did not submit applications to these institutions and the inquiries appeared without authorization, then the inquiry itself is potentially impermissible under 15 U.S.C. § 1681b and should be disputed with TransUnion and Experian as unauthorized pulls.

The team will produce adverse action notice request letters to each institution upon the operator's confirmation that applications were submitted. The team will alternatively produce unauthorized inquiry dispute letters to the bureaus if the operator confirms that no applications were submitted.

State which scenario applies for each institution and the team produces the corresponding instruments immediately.
