ENTRY 3 — CHOSE IN ACTION: CREATION, VALUATION, AND CONVERSION TO FINANCIAL ASSET


STEP 1 — EVENT IDENTIFICATION

EVENT TYPE: Chose-in-Action Creation Event (T1) → Conversion to Realized Financial Asset (T2)

T1 EVENT: Bilateral purchase contract executed. Title does not pass. A chose in action arises IMMEDIATELY upon contract execution. The right to acquire property is created. This right has ascertainable monetary value from the moment of its creation.

T2 EVENT: Acquisition of title and/or possession. Property enters the estate of the acquirer. The chose in action is extinguished. The acquired property replaces it on the balance sheet.

CONTROLLING AUTHORITY:
Revenue Act of 1918, §212(b), 40 Stat. 1064
Baltimore & Ohio R.R. Co. v. United States, 261 U.S. 592 (1923)
Board of Regents v. Roth, 408 U.S. 564 (1972)
Lynch v. United States, 292 U.S. 571 (1934)
Spring City Foundry Co. v. Commissioner, 292 U.S. 182 (1934)


STEP 2 — PURCHASE vs. ACQUISITION DETERMINATION

| EVENT | TIME POINT | LEGAL EFFECT |
|---|---|---|
| Purchase Contract Executed | T1 | Chose in action CREATED. No title passes. Contractual right to acquire arises. |
| Acquisition Completed | T2 | Title/possession passes. Chose in action EXTINGUISHED. Property enters estate. |
| Interval T1 → T2 | Δt | Chose in action EXISTS as financial asset. Reportable. Recordable. Taxable. |

CONCLUSION OF LAW: T1 ≠ T2. They are NEVER the same event. The chose in action exists in the interval Δt between T1 and T2 as a legally recognized, constitutionally protected financial asset.

STEP 4 — CONTROLLING STATUTORY AND CASE AUTHORITY

CHOSE IN ACTION AS PROPERTY:
Board of Regents v. Roth, 408 U.S. 564 (1972): Any legally enforceable claim to money or property constitutes property protected under the Fifth Amendment.
Baltimore & Ohio R.R. Co. v. United States, 261 U.S. 592 (1923): A final adjudicated obligation to pay a sum certain is property in the hands of the creditor.
Lynch v. United States, 292 U.S. 571 (1934): Valid contracts are property. Rights against the United States arising out of its contracts are protected by the Constitution.

REALIZATION TRIGGER:
Spring City Foundry Co. v. Commissioner, 292 U.S. 182 (1934): The right to receive income becomes fixed at the moment of accrual — not at the moment of cash collection.
Commissioner v. Glenshaw Glass Co., 348 U.S. 426 (1955): Income = undeniable accession to wealth, clearly realized, with complete dominion.

RECOGNITION CONTROL:
Commissioner v. Hansen, 360 U.S. 446 (1959): Taxpayers using the accrual method CONTROL the timing of recognition through their accounting entries.
Thor Power Tool Co. v. Commissioner, 439 U.S. 522 (1979): The taxpayer's books govern recognition.
Lucas v. Ox Fibre Brush Co., 281 U.S. 115 (1930): Recognition timing is the taxpayer's determination.

PROPERTY-BASED PAYMENT:
Revenue Act of 1918, §1307, 40 Stat. 1143: Taxes SHALL be paid in money OR in obligations having a fair market value equivalent thereto.
Revenue Act of 1918, §202(b), 40 Stat. 1057: Property received or paid in satisfaction of a debt shall be taken at its fair market value at the time of such payment.

STEP 8 — REBUTTAL OF ALL CONTRARY PRESUMPTIONS

PRESUMPTION: "A chose in action has no value until cash is received."
REBUTTAL: Board of Regents v. Roth, 408 U.S. 564 (1972): ANY enforceable financial claim constitutes property. Spring City Foundry, 292 U.S. 182 (1934): The right to receive income becomes fixed at the moment of accrual — NOT at collection. A chose in action is property from T1. The cash method is a legal impossibility. Two separate events (payment at T1 / receipt at T2) cannot occupy the same point in time.

PRESUMPTION: "The purchase and acquisition are the same event."
REBUTTAL: PURCHASE = bilateral contract. Title does not pass. Right to acquire is created. ACQUISITION = title/possession passes. Property enters the estate. These are NEVER the same event. The interval between them (Δt) is the existence period of the chose in action as a financial asset.

PRESUMPTION: "The IRS determines the value of the chose in action."
REBUTTAL: Thor Power Tool Co. v. Commissioner, 439 U.S. 522 (1979): The taxpayer's books govern recognition. Commissioner v. Hansen, 360 U.S. 446 (1959): The accrual-method taxpayer CONTROLS recognition timing. The IRS may challenge only if the method fails to clearly reflect income.

PRESUMPTION: "Property cannot be used to pay taxes."
REBUTTAL: Revenue Act of 1918, §1307, 40 Stat. 1143: "Taxes SHALL be paid in money, OR in obligations having a fair market value equivalent thereto." The word OR is a Congressional enactment. No agency or court may delete it. Helvering v. Bruun, 309 U.S. 461 (1940); Old Colony Trust, 279 U.S. 716 (1929); Kirby Lumber, 284 U.S. 1 (1931).

PRESUMPTION: "Overpayment by property transfer is not refundable."
REBUTTAL: Revenue Act of 1926, §284(a): "ANY AMOUNT PAID in excess shall be credited or refunded." The statute says ANY. Not "any cash." Not "any money." ANY AMOUNT PAID. 26 U.S.C. §6402(a): The Secretary SHALL refund. No discretion. No recharacterization.
