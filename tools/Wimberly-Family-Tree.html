Verification Pass — 10 November 2026

Each maternal and Washington–Dandy branch was checked against published genealogies and record abstracts available online. Result: the New England portion of the tree is real and well documented, but its attachment to your family is not confirmed.

  • Confirmed (applied): the Tuttle–Todd line from Simon Tuttle of Ringstead (c. 1560) down to Sybil Todd (1740), and the Talmadge line from Thomas Talmage the immigrant (c. 1580) down to Samuel Talmadge (b. 2 Feb 1760).
  • Corrections applied: a missing generation — Josiah Talmadge Sr. (bp. 1713) and Phebe Dibble — now sits between John Talmage and Josiah (b. 1736), which resolves the 1723/1736 date conflict. Stephen Todd's father is Samuel Todd; Sybil Todd's mother is Lydia Ives; Joseph Tuttle's mother is Elizabeth; Hannah Munson's parents are Capt. Thomas and Joanna Munson.
  • Break in the chain: the documented Samuel Talmadge (b. 1760, son of Josiah and Sybil) married Lucretia Todd, had children Enos Todd, Davis and Lucretia, and died in 1844. The tree's "Dr. Samuel Talmage 1760–1853" with wife Ann Fry Frey and daughter Caroline (b. 1805) does not match him — two different men appear to have been merged. Until Caroline's father is proven, nothing above her belongs to your line.
  • Contradicted: the "Elizabeth Bancroft (1590–1660)" hint — the immigrant Thomas Talmage's wife is unnamed in every record. Thomas Osborn and Mary Goatly were a real couple, but their daughter Rebecca is recorded as c. 1642–1704, not the Rebecca born 1658.
  • No source found: Southcott, Totehyll, Mason, Barnhouse, De Elmham, Bryan and Bohython entries; Lieutenant Miller and Elizabeth Dimon as Experience Miller's parents.
  • Not verifiable online: Washington, Dandy, Moore, Huntley, Robinson, Lomax, Henry and most Quivers entries (1804–1960). These need census, vital and probate records. One lead: a Coy Quivers in Charles City County, Virginia records.

Quivers lead: Charles City County, Virginia records describe a Coy Quivers — son of Jonathan & Sarah Quivers and brother of Emanuel — who was enslaved until he bought his freedom in 1848 from Abby Nelson of Clarke County for $560 (deed recorded in Petersburg, Virginia, Deed Book 16, p. 688), then moved to Pennsylvania and outlived three wives. The name is rare, but no dates or children are given, so the match to Coy Quivers (1804–1899) is unproven. The same records list Jonathan as a blacksmith and a member of Charles City Baptist Church (roll begun c. 1820), with five children: Sarah "Polly" Kemp, Lucy, Eleanor, Coy and Emanuel. Emanuel (1814–1879) was born at Berkeley Plantation and later settled in California. The Quivers Family Notes typescript at the county's Richard M. Bowman Center for Local History (10790 Courthouse Road, Charles City, VA 23030; 804-652-1516) is the next source to check.
